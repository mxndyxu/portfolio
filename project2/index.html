Assignment 4 Persuasive or Deceptive Visualization?

Mandy Xu — max002@ucsd.edu

Proposition: Limiting abortion services is effective in reducing the amount of abortions

FOR the Proposition

Design Decisions and Rationale:

  • Y-axis truncation creates a deceptive visual slope of the data
  • Visually exaggerating difference in abortion rates to make negative correlation more pronounced
  • Assertive title frames the relationship as established

AGAINST the Proposition

Design Decisions and Rationale:

  • Displays moderate pearson correlation
  • Begins both axes at 0
  • Uses a broader y-axis range to show the true linear association visually

Final Reflection

Creating these two visualizations showed me how strongly design choices can influence the interpretatin of data. The FOR visuaization uses a truncated y-axis and assertive framing to exaggerate the negative relationship, while the AGAINST visualization uses a broader axis range to make the trend appear more true to the data. Although the AGAINST visualization still shows a slight negative trend, it emphasizes that the association is at most moderate based on the displayed Pearson's correlation.

I define ethical visualization as presenting data persuasively without distorting what it actually demonstrates. I believe choices such as higlighting patterns are acceptable but exaggerating trends through misleading scales or presenting corrleation as causation is wrong. Persuasion does not require complete neutrality, it requires transparency about design decisions and honestly about the data's limitations.
